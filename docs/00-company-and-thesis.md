# Adventure Works Group: company profile and reporting thesis

> **Fictional company.** Adventure Works is Microsoft's sample business. The ledger is Microsoft's AdventureWorksDW finance data (MIT licence), re-dated to a fiscal calendar ending March 2027. Budgets for FY2026-FY2028 are generated by a documented script. Nothing here describes a real company.

## Who they are

Adventure Works Group designs and sells performance bicycles, components and riding apparel through dealers and its own online store. The group parent is Canadian, so it **reports in Canadian dollars** and runs a **fiscal year from 1 April to 31 March**. FY2027 is the year ending 31 March 2027.

| Region | Entities | Currency | Group interest | In the group since |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America | Five US sales divisions (Northeast, Northwest, Central, Southeast, Southwest) | USD | 100% | Before FY2025 |
| North America | Canadian Division | CAD | 75% | Before FY2025 |
| Europe | France | EUR | 50% | April 2025 (FY2026) |
| Europe | Germany | EUR | 25% | April 2025 (FY2026) |
| Pacific | Australia | AUD | 37.5% (a 50% joint venture held through 75%-owned Pacific Operations) | April 2026 (FY2027) |

"Group interest" is the effective economic interest (ownership multiplied down the entity hierarchy). Partly owned entities are fully consolidated; the share of profit belonging to minority partners is shown as **non-controlling interest (NCI)**.

## Three years in brief

**FY2025: North American core.** Six entities, one region. The FY2025 budget is the original Adventure Works budget.

**FY2026: European entry.** France and Germany joined at the start of the year through partnership agreements, after the FY2026 budget had been set from FY2025 actuals. Their results therefore ran against no budget in their first year. An ERP migration in August 2025 left about 3% of that month's postings without a department.

**FY2027: Pacific entry and digital spend.** An Australian joint venture began trading in April 2026, again outside the approved budget. Finance opened a new GL account for software subscriptions as cloud tools replaced professional services. The September 2026 ledger was the first exported from the new ERP version, with a different file layout.

The data-quality layer catches each of these source-system effects; see `docs/eda/eda_report.md`.

## The question the CFO is asking

The October 2026 close (month 7 of FY2027) shows revenue well ahead of budget. The board will hear "we beat budget". The CFO needs to know whether that is true in the way that matters.

The pack is built to answer four questions, in order:

1. **Is growth organic or bought?** Separate like-for-like entities from those that joined this year. Budget variance on unbudgeted entities is not operating outperformance.
2. **Is cost growth eating the upside?** Revenue and costs move together across the group (Spearman 0.94 for cost of sales, 0.66 for operating expenses). Where opex grows faster than revenue, operating leverage is negative.
3. **Where will FY2027 land?** Rolling outlook: seven months of actuals plus five months of budget, compared with the full-year budget.
4. **Is the balance sheet funding the growth?** Cash, working-capital days (receivables, inventory, payables) and leverage at period end.

## What the EDA already tells us (FY2027 year to date, Apr-Oct 2026, CAD at monthly average rates)

- Revenue is up **29.8%** on the same seven months of FY2026; **like-for-like growth is 23.1%**. Australia adds CAD 1.9M with no budget behind it.
- Cost of sales tracks revenue almost one-for-one, so gross margin is structurally stable; the profit story is decided below gross profit.
- Monthly group revenue is normally distributed. Ledger line amounts are heavily skewed, so anomaly detection uses robust (median and MAD) statistics.
- Revenue is seasonal: May, June, August and February run 20-50% above an average month; October and January run 30-40% below.

## How each report page serves the thesis

| Page | Question | Primary view |
|---|---|---|
| Company Overview | Who are we and what changed? | Perimeter timeline, entity map, headline KPIs |
| Executive Summary | Are we on plan? | KPI cards vs budget and prior year, EBITDA bridge, top exceptions |
| Budget vs Actual | Where are the variances? | Goodly rolling actual/budget view, variance by line, entity and department |
| Income Statement | How did each line perform? | Dynamic P&L with variance, growth and % of revenue |
| Balance Sheet and Cash | Is the group liquid and solvent? | Closing balances, working-capital days, leverage |
| Outlook | Where will the year land? | Actual + remaining budget vs full-year budget |
| Data Quality | Can I trust it? | File log, injected-vs-detected reconciliation, integrity checks |
